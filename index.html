When it gets frustrating

Football is 85% mental and 15% physical.

QBs who rise to the occasion understand this. You can too, Max. You just need to do it consistently.

Remember: everyone knows that not getting practice reps or game reps is frustrating, and it creates uncertainty about future playing time, especially when you've been given a scholarship and told you'll get game reps eventually. It's frustrating when you have a bad practice, throw an INT, misread a defense, run the wrong play, or get called out for not focusing and the coaches get on you for it. It has happened, and it will probably happen again.

What matters is how you deal with adversity. Not just one day, but every day.

How you handle running the wrong play, missing the correct read or throwing an INT: how do you respond next? Next play, next rep, next practice? It all counts, and it's all being evaluated. Can you be mentally tough enough to wipe that bad pass or bad practice clean and start over with a positive attitude and top effort?

Are you staying mentally focused in practice and in meetings when you're not "the guy" and you're a backup QB? Can you force yourself to truly focus in those meetings, practices and games when meetings get boring, practice becomes routine, and you know you're not going to play in the upcoming game?

Attitude, Effort and Availability are what matter most.

It's not easy, but your path to where you are now wasn't easy either. Remember, you have 4 years of eligibility. You're not just practicing and going to meetings for this year. You're showing the coaches what you can do in year 2, year 3 and beyond. Coaches want to see improvement and growth, especially in young QBs. Mistakes happen and bad practices happen. It's what you do after that the coaches want to see.

You have serious gifts, Max, and you've already come a long way, beating the odds to be a college QB. Don't let the routine frustrations of a bad play, an INT or a bad practice affect your overall desire and commitment to be the best QB you can be. You've already shown you belong and can play QB at a high level. Don't allow anyone or anything to stop you from reaching your long-term goal of being a starting QB.

You got this. You are there. Focus on what you can control, grow with the team, and you will realize your goals.
